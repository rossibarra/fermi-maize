Genomic Length Scales (smallest → largest)

  • Conserved noncoding sequence (CNS): ∼30 bp (median 12–40 bp across species; 73% upstream of their gene, ∼25% >25 kb away)43; 27 bp–15 kb in maize42
  • Exon / intron: ∼150 bp each (160 bp exon, 140 bp intron medians; 90% of introns < 1.4 kb)1
  • TIR transposon copy: ∼300 bp (310 bp median)7
  • Coding sequence (CDS) per gene: ∼900 bp (880 bp median; 3 exons/gene median; 23% single-exon)1
  • Pre-mRNA gene span: ∼3 kb (2.7 kb median; 90% < 9 kb)1
  • Intact TE copy: ∼10 kb (LTR retrotransposons 11 kb; Helitrons 7 kb medians)7
  • Intergenic distance: ∼25 kb (26 kb median; 10–90%: 0.8–136 kb)1
  • Extent of LD (distance to mean r2 = 0.1): ∼10–50 kb in annual teosintes (e.g. parviglumis n = 70, mexicana n = 81) vs. ∼200 kb in 507 maize inbreds15; within genes, r2 < 0.1 by 1.5 kb in 102 diverse inbreds (6 genes)14
  • Distal regulatory elements: ∼2–100 kb from genes (∼1/3 of accessible chromatin >2 kb from a gene10; tb1 ∼60 kb11, Vgt1 70 kb12, b1 100 kb13)
  • Domestication sweep: ∼300 kb (mean selected region)16
  • Centromere (CENH3 domain): ∼2 Mb (1.8 Mb)17
  • Chromosome length: ∼200 Mb (152–308 Mb; ∼16 genes/Mb median, 35/Mb near tips vs. 10/Mb mid-chromosome)1

Genome Composition, Variation & Selection

  • Genome fractions: ∼85% TEs (∼74% LTR, ∼8.5% DNA TEs)2,6; 200k intact copies in 29k families; huck, ji, cinful-zeon, opie 7–11% each7,8; ∼8% genic; ∼2% CDS1; 42% callable across NAM assemblies18.
  • Conserved noncoding sequence: ∼5% of the genome (∼107 Mb conserved across Andropogoneae, 42 Mb in all species; ∼half in UTRs and introns; 87% of genes have a CNS within 2 kb upstream)42; 80% of maize CNSs carry a predicted TF binding site43.
  • Protein evidence: peptides detected for 66% of annotated B73 proteins across public mass-spec data44; proteins from ∼18,500 genes in a 33-tissue atlas45.
  • Paleopolyploidy: ancient allotetraploid (subgenomes split ∼12 MYA31; polyploidy shared with Tripsacum15); ≥50% of duplicate genes lost9.
  • Polymorphism & SVs: ∼1 SNP / 70 bp between two random haplotypes18; >83M SNPs (1,218 lines)19; ∼8 × 105 SVs (>100 bp) in 26 NAM founders (50% < 5 kb)2.
  • Divergence & admixture: landraces retain ∼83% of parviglumis π (FST ≈ 0.11)16; parviglumis vs. mexicana FST ≈ 0.3320; modern maize carries ∼18% (15–25%) mexicana ancestry21.
  • Selection & constraint: background selection reduces π ∼43% at genes (B = 0.57; recovers by 0.1 cM) and ∼26% genome-wide (mean B = 0.74)18,27; only ∼10% of the genome (>1 cM/Mb) nears neutral π30. Deleterious rate ∼3 × 10−10 bp−1 (Ud ≈ 0.6 per gamete; u/μ ≈ 0.5–1%, an equivalent target of ∼10–20 Mb)18. Domestication s ≈ 0.01516.

Evolutionary Timeline (approximate)

  • ∼12 MYA: maize–sorghum & subgenome split31 • <3 MYA: LTR expansion32 (median intact LTR ∼0.8 MY7,22)
  • ∼650 kya: Zea–Tripsacum15 → ∼120 kya: Z. luxurians15 → ∼30–60 kya: parviglumis–mexicana split15,33
  • ∼9 kya: Balsas domestication34 (microfossils 8.7 kya35; cobs 6.25 kya36) → ∼6.5 kya: S. America37 → ∼5 kya: mexicana admixture21 → ∼4.1 kya: US Southwest38 → 1493 CE: Europe39

Notes on Derived & Computed Values

  • Genome size & DNA mass: landrace 2C = 4.62–6.29 pg3,4; haploid size = 2C/2 × 0.978 Gb/pg = 2.26–3.08 Gb (B73: 2.13 Gb ≈ 2.18 pg per 1C).
  • Mutations & crossovers per gamete: μ × 2.13 × 109 bp for μ = 3–7.3 × 10−822,23; 7.3 × 10−8 is from unpublished mutation-accumulation lines. The NAM consensus map has 1,478 markers at 1-cM spacing across 10 chromosomes (∼1,470 cM)25,26; map/100 ≈ 15 COs/gamete; 1,470 cM / 2,132 Mb ≈ 0.69 cM/Mb.
  • Gene & TE annotations: canonical transcripts of 39,756 protein-coding genes in Zm-B73-REFERENCE-NAM-5.0_Zm00001eb.1.gff31; density in 2,128 1-Mb windows (“tips”/“mid-chromosome” = outer/inner 10% of each chromosome half). Intact TEs (198,875) from Zm-B73-REFERENCE-GRAMENE-4.0.TEs.1.0.gff3 (2,106 Mb v4)7,8; nested TEs overlap.
  • Diversity, θW, Ne, B: across 904,011,088 callable sites (42% of B73 v5; ≤6 of 25 non-B73 NAM founders missing)18. Fitting π = π0e−u/r gives π0 = 0.0189 (single-locus asymptote 0.0212) and mean B = 0.7418,27. θW = (51,554,740 / 904,011,088) / a26 (a26 = 3.82). Ne = π0/4μ; equivalent target fraction = u/μ. Masked regions (56%) are high-recombination and low-constraint, so π, Ne and u are probably underestimates.
  • Demography & census: ARG Ne trajectory read from a plot (±10–20%)28; ∼85% bottleneck over ∼2,500 years, ending near the earliest archaeological maize at Xihuatoxtla35. Census: 47.9M ha of open-pollinated maize × ∼25,000 plants/ha29.
  • LTR ages: T = K/2μ (K = 1 − LTR identity; μ = 3.3 × 10−8/bp/yr22; 128,481 elements7; median identity 94.9%; 62% < 1 MY). The 85% identity cutoff truncates ages at ∼2.3 MY.

Worked Fermi Math Examples in Maize Genetics

Back-of-the-envelope calculations showing how the Top 10 constants and genomic length scales combine to answer practical biological, evolutionary and experimental questions.

1. How many paired-end short reads are needed for 10× sequencing coverage?
10×(2×109 bp)2×150 bp/pair=2×1010 bp300 bp/pair≈ 7 × 107 read pairs (70 million pairs)
Only ∼42% of the genome (∼900 Mb) aligns cleanly across assembled NAM genomes; most of the rest is repetitive TE sequence. Expect usable ∼10× depth over roughly that fraction, while reads from high-copy TE families pile up or map ambiguously.
2. What is the global mutation supply per site each crop generation?
(1012 OP plants) × (2 gametes/plant) × (3 × 10−8 mutations/bp/gamete) ≈ 6 × 104 transmitted substitutions per site per generation
This is the expected number of new mutations transmitted at a site in one crop generation, averaged across sites, under the stated census and mutation rate. For adaptation, the useful next calculation is 2Nμtarget in the population under selection, where μtarget is the rate to the relevant allele or set of alleles.
3. How many differences do you expect in a typical gene between two random inbred lines?
(3 × 103 bp gene span) × (πgenic ≈ 1.2 × 10−2) ≈ 36 expected differences per gene
A rough estimate for a 3 kb gene at the reported genic π. The ∼11 differences you would get for the ∼900 bp CDS is an equal-diversity benchmark; for a coding estimate, use CDS-specific π. The genic mean is not an upper bound for any individual gene.
4. How long until recombination splits a 100 kb segment?
rblock = (0.1 Mb) × (1 cM/Mb) = 0.1 cM = 10−3 crossovers/gen → T≈1rblock ≈ 1,000 generations
This is the mean waiting time for a recombination event across 100 kb along one lineage at 1 cM/Mb; at the measured mean of 0.7 cM/Mb it is ∼1,400 generations.
5. How many new deleterious mutations enter the genome per gamete?
Ud = (2 × 109 bp) × (3 × 10−10 deleterious mutations/bp) ≈ 0.6 deleterious mutations/gamete under the fitted model
Dividing the modeled deleterious rate by the total substitution rate gives u/μ ≈ 0.5–1%, an equivalent target size of ∼10–20 Mb under the model. This does not identify a physical set of bases at which every possible substitution is deleterious.
6. How many crossovers occur in a 100-plant F2 mapping population, and how far apart are they?
(100 F2 plants) × (2 gametes/plant) × (15 COs/gamete) = 3,000 COs → 2.13×109 bp3,000 COs ≈ 0.7 Mb between breakpoints
About 0.7 Mb is the mean physical spacing between pooled crossover breakpoints. Bin size sets the limit on mapping resolution; QTL support intervals from 100 plants are usually far wider.
7. How big a field plot is needed to grow 10,000 plants for a selection experiment?
Area=104 plants3×104 plants/ha ≈ 0.33 hectares (∼0.8 acres, or a ∼55 m × 60 m plot)
At ∼500 kernels per ear, harvesting the top 1% (100 ears) yields ∼50,000 seeds, more than enough to replant the next cycle at the same population size.
